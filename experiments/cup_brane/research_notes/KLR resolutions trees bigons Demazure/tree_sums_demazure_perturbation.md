# Tree sums, Kirchhoff/Symanzik polynomials and Demazure operators on products of Q_ij: homological perturbation, deformed KLR algebras, and the corrected differential of complexes of projectives

Conventions in these notes. "Verified" means the statement was checked against the paper's text (ar5iv/arXiv HTML) or a primary abstract during this session. Items recalled from memory but not re-checked are listed only under **Gaps**, as unverified pointers. "Observed structure" means the empirical cup-brane formula in the brief: a rooted tree sum with edge weights r_c t_{cc'}, and for cyclic G a spanning-tree sum times prod_{e not in T} Q_e.

## Q1. Homological perturbation lemma and A-infinity homotopy transfer: the precise tree formulas, deformations of the algebra, curved perturbations, and loops

### Takeaway
The basic perturbation lemma gives the perturbed differential as a **linear path sum** b + p delta (h delta)^n i. **Trees** appear only when the structure being transferred is multilinear (A∞-algebras, categories, modules), as in Kadeishvili, Merkulov, Kontsevich–Soibelman §6.4 and Markl Prop. 6. **Graphs with loops** appear exactly in the "quantum" or BV settings: quantum L∞ algebras, algebras over modular operads, and wheeled PROPs, where a trace or contraction (BV Laplacian / divergence) is available. None of the sources found treats "complex of projectives over a deformed algebra" as a named theorem with a tree formula. The closest framework is solving a Maurer–Cartan (MC) equation with curvature, together with Lowen's obstruction theory (see Q5).

### Cited Findings
- **Crainic, "On the perturbation lemma, and deformations", arXiv:math/0403266 (2004).** Def. 2.1 sets up homotopy-equivalence (HE) data: complexes (L,b), (M,b), quasi-isomorphisms i: L→M, p: M→L, and a homotopy h with i p = 1 + b h + h b. Unlike a deformation retract, p i = 1 is **not** required. Def. 2.2: a perturbation δ of b on M satisfies (b+δ)^2 = 0, and it is *small* if (1 − δh) is invertible. Put A = (1 − δh)^{-1} δ. Then eq. (2) gives **i_1 = i + hAi, p_1 = p + pAh, h_1 = h + hAh, b_1 = b + pAi**. Thm. 2.4 (Main Perturbation Lemma): the perturbed data are again HE data. Auxiliary identities (3)–(5) are δhA = Ahδ = A − δ, (1−δh)^{-1} = 1 + Ah, and AipA + Ab + bA = 0. — [Crainic, arXiv:math/0403266](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0403266) (read via [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/math/0403266))
- Crainic, Remark 2.3(iii): the classical lemma assumes a filtration lowered by δ. This makes δh locally nilpotent, so δ is small. That is the formal or "graded" convergence mechanism. — [Crainic, arXiv:math/0403266](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0403266)
- Crainic §3: 3.4, for a contractible (M,b) with contraction h, the perturbed contraction is H = h(1 − δh)^{-1}. 3.5, double complexes: i_1 = Σ(hδ)^n i, h_1 = Σ h(δh)^n. 3.7, a degree-k contraction survives perturbation if 1 − δh is invertible in degrees k, k+1. — [Crainic, arXiv:math/0403266](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0403266)
- Crainic §4 deforms **Lie algebra brackets**: the Chevalley–Eilenberg differential for [·,·]_t is written as d + δ_t, Cor. 3.7 is applied, and Thm. 4.1 says H^2(g;g) = 0 implies smooth deformations are trivial. §5 treats associative (Banach) algebra deformations via Hochschild cohomology, e.g. Prop. 5.2. **The paper does not treat deforming modules or complexes over a deformed algebra, nor curved (non-flat) perturbations.** Every perturbation must satisfy (b+δ)^2 = 0. — [Crainic, arXiv:math/0403266](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0403266)
- **Markl, "Transferring A∞ (strongly homotopy associative) structures", arXiv:math/0401007.** Eq. (1) gives the transferred data: ν_n = f∘p_n∘g^{⊗n} (structure), φ_n = f∘q_n and ψ_n = h∘p_n∘g^{⊗n} (A∞-morphisms extending f and g), and H_n = h∘q_n (A∞-homotopy extending h). **Prop. 6:** p_n = Σ_{T∈P_n} (−1)^{ϑ(T)} F_T. Here P_n is the set of planar directed trees with at least binary vertices and n leaves; vertices are decorated by μ_k and interior edges by h. The sign is ϑ(T) = Σ_v ϑ(r_1,…,r_k), with ϑ(u_1,…,u_s) = Σ_{α<β} u_α(u_β+1). Inductive form, eq. (7): p_2 = μ_2 and p_n = Σ ± μ_k(h p_{r_1} ⊗ … ⊗ h p_{r_k}). Thm. 5 says these solve the transfer problem. Prop. 7 is a tree formula for q_n with edges decorated by h and by g∘f. — [Markl, arXiv:math/0401007](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0401007) (via [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/math/0401007))
- Markl, Remark 4: under the side conditions fh = 0, hg = 0, h^2 = 0, the Huebschmann–Kadeishvili coalgebra perturbation lemma gives a solution, and the p-kernel corresponds to Σ(h̃ δ_μ)^n. In words, the tree sum is the HPL path sum applied on the bar construction. Markl makes no uniqueness claim. Props. 10–12 cover inverses and extending homotopies. — [Markl, arXiv:math/0401007](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0401007)
- **Kontsevich–Soibelman, "Homological mirror symmetry and torus fibrations", arXiv:math/0011041, §6.4 "A∞-structure on a subcomplex".** Setup: Π is an idempotent commuting with d = m_1, with 1 − Π = dH + Hd and Π = i∘p. Then m_1^B = p m_1 i, m_2^B = p m_2 (i⊗i), and m_n^B = Σ_T ± m_{n,T} for n ≥ 3, summed over planar trees with n+1 tails. Leaves carry i, vertices m_k, **internal edges H** (a new vertex on each internal edge), and the root p. Thm. 3: this is an A∞-structure. Prop. 7: a canonical A∞ quasi-isomorphism g: B → A is given by the same trees with **H at the root instead of p**. Remark 17(a): the same works for A∞-categories. The roadmap (§1.2) says these restate Merkulov's formulas as planar-tree sums. — [Kontsevich–Soibelman, arXiv:math/0011041](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0011041) (via [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/math/0011041))
- The tree formula for A∞ is attributed to [Kad80, Thm. 1] (Kadeishvili), with [KS00, Sect. 6.4] as the explicit tree formula. — [Robert-Nicoud–Wierstra? "Operads and Maurer–Cartan spaces", arXiv:1807.02129](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.02129). The authors of 1807.02129 were not verified; the quoted attribution sentence was.
- Loday–Vallette, *Algebraic Operads* (Grundlehren 346, 2012), §10.3 is the operadic homotopy transfer section; Drummond-Cole–Vallette cite [LV10, §10.3] for the failure of homotopy transfer for BV algebras. - **Loops, case 1: quantum L∞ / BV.** Doubek–Jurčo–Pulmann, "Quantum L∞ algebras and the homological perturbation lemma", arXiv:1712.02696, Comm. Math. Phys. 367 (2019) 215–240. Quantum L∞ algebras carry a scalar product and operations indexed by **higher-genus graphs**. The minimal model is built with the HPL and is a **Feynman-diagram expansion**, i.e. the effective action in finite-dimensional BV. They also construct the homotopy to the effective algebra. — [arXiv:1712.02696](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.02696)
- **Loops, case 2: modular operads.** Chuang–Lazarev, "Feynman diagrams and minimal models for operadic algebras", arXiv:0802.3507, J. LMS 81 (2010) 317–337. For algebras over the cobar construction of a dg operad, the minimal model has **structure maps given by sums over decorated trees**. For algebras over **modular** operads, "the sums over trees get replaced by sums over general Feynman graphs". - Braun–Maunder, "Minimal models of quantum homotopy Lie algebras via the BV-formalism", arXiv:1703.00082, is another source for loop-order minimal models (title verified; content not read). — [arXiv:1703.00082](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1703.00082)
- **Loops, case 3: wheeled PROPs, i.e. directed graphs with oriented cycles.** Markl–Merkulov–Shadrin, "Wheeled PROPs, graph complexes and the master equation", arXiv:math/0610683, JPAA 213 (2009) 496–535. They extend PROPs to handle **traces**. Free objects are built from directed graphs that may contain **wheels** (directed closed loops), and this describes master equations with **divergence operators**. They build minimal wheeled resolutions of Com and Ass, which mix associahedra and cyclohedra. Survey: Merkulov arXiv:0911.3321. — [arXiv:math/0610683](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0610683); [Merkulov, arXiv:0911.3321](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0911.3321)
- Homotopy transfer on non-compact manifolds with boundary yields Feynman-graph formulae, and with boundary the effective theory may land in "derived BV algebras". — [Wang–Yan(?), arXiv:2203.09071](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.09071). Author names are from search snippets.

### Inferences
- **Path versus tree.** The HPL output b_1 = b + Σ_{n≥0} p δ(hδ)^n i is a sum over *linear chains* of perturbation insertions. Trees arise when the object being corrected is **nonlinear in the unknowns**. The cleanest instance is the MC equation (d_0 + x)^2 = 0, i.e. κ + [d_0, x] + x^2 = 0 with κ := d_0^2. Solving iteratively with a contracting homotopy, x = −h(κ + x^2), produces **rooted binary trees with leaves κ**. Each leaf is one "defect" of d_0^2. In the observed structure each tree edge carries a factor r_c t_{cc'}, which is a braid-relation defect (see Q3). So the tree edges plausibly correspond to κ-insertions, and the tree records the nesting of corrections. This is an analogy, not a theorem from the sources.
- The rooted structure, with the root being the moving strand, fits the A∞-*module* version of transfer: trees hang off a distinguished spine (the module input). The KS §6.4 / Markl formulas specialise to modules by marking one leaf-to-root path. I did not verify a published module tree formula in this session.
- **Loops.** In every source found, terms with a cycle require an extra contraction or trace (BV Δ, modular-operad gluing, wheeled trace). The observed bigon factor Q_e for each non-tree edge is a natural candidate for this "self-contraction": a strand crosses another and comes back, closing a cycle in G. In the matrix-tree language of Q4, the non-tree edges correspond to Symanzik-polynomial variables (loop momenta), so "spanning tree × ∏_{e∉T} Q_e" is literally a first-Symanzik/Kirchhoff structure.
- Uniqueness in the fully graded setting is not part of the HPL. It comes from vanishing of the obstruction and ambiguity groups in the relevant bidegree (see Q5).

- No paper was found that states a tree-sum formula for the **corrected differential of a complex of projective modules when the algebra is deformed**. I believe this is not a named result in the HPL literature.
- Loday–Vallette theorem numbers (HTT for A∞ in Ch. 9, operadic HTT and tree formula in §10.3) were not verified from the book.
- From memory, not verified this session: Kadeishvili 1980 (Russian Math. Surveys 35); Merkulov "Strong homotopy algebras of a Kähler manifold" (IMRN 1999); Huebschmann–Kadeishvili "Small models for chain algebras" (Math. The specific "one-loop" formulas in Doubek–Jurčo–Pulmann and Braun–Maunder were not read; theorem numbers are missing.

## Q2. Iterated cones, twisted complexes, homotopy-coherent cubes: are the higher homotopies sums over trees?

### Takeaway
For hypercubes of chain complexes (Manolescu–Ozsváth link surgery, Zemke, HHSZ), both the structure maps and the hypercube perturbation lemma are **sums over increasing chains in the cube**, i.e. paths. The same holds for twisted-complex and box-tensor differentials, which are sums over sequences of δ-insertions. Trees appear only once an A∞ structure is itself transferred. The closest "curved complex corrected by higher homotopies" result is the Eisenbud–Shamash system of higher homotopies. That system is indexed by multi-indices, not trees.

### Cited Findings
- **Zemke, "Bordered manifolds with torus boundary and the link surgery formula", arXiv:2109.11520.** Def. 2.5: an n-dimensional hypercube of chain complexes is (C_ε, D_{ε,ε'} for ε ≤ ε'), satisfying the compatibility (2.2), i.e. Σ_{ε≤ε'≤ε''} D_{ε',ε''} D_{ε,ε'} = 0. Def. 2.6 defines hyperboxes, and §2.3 compresses them into hypercubes by composing along axes (Lemma 2.8: independent of axis order up to homotopy). Eq. (2.6) writes hypercube structure maps as **sums over increasing chains** ε = ε_1 < … < ε_i = ε' of polygon maps. §3.3: the box-tensor differential is Σ_j (id ⊗ m_{j+1}) ∘ δ^j, a sum over sequences. §4 "Homological perturbation theory" contains 4.1 A∞-modules, 4.2 DA-bimodules, 4.3 Hypercubes, 4.4 Hypercubes of DA-bimodules; only the headings were read. — [Zemke, arXiv:2109.11520](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.11520)
- A search snippet says Zemke reviews "a version of the homological perturbation lemma for hypercubes, from [HHSZ22], where a proof may be found". The lattice-homology paper restates it as "similar to work of Huebschmann–Kadeishvili [HK91]", which gives canonical hypercube structure maps on the transferred data. — [Zemke, arXiv:2111.14962](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.14962); [arXiv:2109.11520](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.11520). The expansion of "HHSZ" in the snippet ("Hayden, Hedden, Sarkar, Zemke") is unverified and possibly wrong.
- Zemke 2111.14962 §1.2 uses homological perturbation to show the Heegaard Floer complex is homotopy equivalent to "the lattice complex with extra (higher length) differentials added". This is the same phenomenon as an undeformed differential plus higher corrections. — [arXiv:2111.14962](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.14962)
- Manolescu–Ozsváth, "Heegaard Floer homology and integer surgeries on links", arXiv:1011.1317: the link surgery formula is a hypercube of chain complexes, "in some sense also created by an iterated mapping cone construction", generalising the Ozsváth–Szabó knot surgery formula. — [arXiv:1011.1317](https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.1317)
- Hedden–Kim–Mark–Park (two-bridge link surgeries) state a lemma on composing homotopy equivalences and a hypercube version. — [arXiv:1402.5727](https://arxiv.org/abs/1402.5727)
- **Eisenbud–Shamash.** Let G be a free resolution over S of a module annihilated by f_1,…,f_c. A *system of higher homotopies* is a family σ_a, indexed by multi-indices a, with σ_0 = differential. Such a system always exists, and when f is a regular sequence Sh(G,σ) is an R = S/(f)-free resolution. Shamash did c = 1 (1969) and Eisenbud the general case. — [Sobieska, arXiv:2212.06999](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.06999); [Macaulay2 docs, EisenbudShamash](https://macaulay2.com/doc/Macaulay2/share/doc/Macaulay2/CompleteIntersectionResolutions/html/___Eisenbud__Shamash.html)

### Inferences
- The observed corrected differential is **not** a hypercube-HPL output. Those are path sums, and the observed formula has genuine branching: a rooted tree on pairs of equal-coloured strands. The branching indicates that corrections are **composed with one another**, which is a quadratic, MC-type recursion, rather than being single long zig-zags.
- **Eisenbud–Shamash is the closest analogue for the "curvature" aspect.** There, d_0^2 = Σ f_i·(higher homotopy) on the lift, which is a matrix-factorisation-type defect. In the observed case, d_0^2 is (r_i t_ij) × (identity diagram) at each triple point. The higher homotopies of Eisenbud–Shamash are indexed by monomials in the "operators" t_i (divided powers), which fixes their combinatorics as multi-indices. The cup-brane tree combinatorics instead comes from the geometry of which strands exchange labels.

### Gaps
- The precise hypercube HPL formula and its source (probably Hendricks–Hom–Stoffregen–Zemke, "Surgery exact triangles in involutive Heegaard Floer homology", arXiv:2011.00113) were not read; that attribution is from memory.
- Not verified this session: Seidel, *Fukaya categories and Picard–Lefschetz theory*, §(3k)–(3l), twisted complexes with μ^1_{Tw} = Σ μ^d(δ,…,δ,a,δ,…,δ); Bondal–Kapranov "Enhanced triangulated categories" (1990); Toledo–Tong twisting cochains; Lipshitz–Ozsváth–Thurston bimodule tree diagrams (arXiv:1003.0598); Rickard complexes and Khovanov homology with higher homotopies. I found no source where homotopy-coherent cube homotopies are given by sums over trees.

## Q3. KLR algebras: polynomial representation, Q_ij with parameters, deformations, and where "Demazure applied to (products of) Q_ij" appears

### Takeaway
"Demazure operator applied to Q_ij" is literally the right-hand side of the (i,j,i) braid relation in Rouquier's definition. For Q_ij(u,v) = t_ij u + t_ji v, the divided difference is the constant t_ij. With crossings of equal colour acting as r_i·∂, the defect becomes r_i t_ij, matching the observed triple-point failure. Products of Q's appear in the squares of KKK intertwiners and in the central element R_{N,M}R_{M,N}. Rouquier explicitly frames quiver Hecke algebras as a flat, filtered family whose associated graded is polynomial ⋊ nilHecke. No source was found that applies Demazure operators to *products* of Q_ij over a graph and obtains spanning-tree sums.

### Inferences
- **Exact match to the triple-point defect.** Take Q_ij(u,v) = t_ij u + t_ji v, where u is the variable of the i-strand. Rouquier's braid RHS for ν = (i,j,i) is the divided difference in the two *outer* i-variables: [Q(x_{a+2},y) − Q(x_a,y)]/(x_{a+2} − x_a) = t_ij, a constant times the identity diagram. If equal-colour crossings are rescaled so that xψ − ψx' = r_i, so that ψ acts by r_i ∂ in the polynomial representation, the braid defect becomes **r_i · ∂(Q_ij) = r_i t_ij**. This is precisely the observed weight per tree edge. **Each tree edge is a "Demazure-of-Q" term from Rouquier's braid relation.**
- **Hand-over terms.** The dot-free "hand-over" terms are the inhomogeneous part of ψ f = s(f) ψ + r ∂(f). KKK's intertwiner φ_a = (x_{a+1} − x_a)τ_a − 1 isolates exactly this "−1" (identity-diagram) term for equal colours, and φ_a^2 = Q + δ records it. So the intertwiner calculus is the standard bookkeeping device for separating crossing terms from hand-over terms. Using φ's instead of τ's (after localising at x_a − x_{a+1}) might diagonalise the observed structure.
- **Deformation parameters as a Rees/filtration parameter.** At r_i = 0, equal-colour crossings commute with dots and the algebra becomes the associated graded (polynomial ⋊ nil-Coxeter type). This matches Rouquier's "filtered with associated graded a wreath product … PBW". At t = 0, bigons vanish. The undeformed d_0 is therefore a complex over the associated graded, and the true d is its filtered lift. This is exactly the setting of Q5: PBW, nonhomogeneous Koszul duality, curvature.
- **Products of Q's under Demazure operators** occur when a j-strand passes several i-strands and back: the bigons multiply ∏_k Q_ij(x_k, y), and closing with a nilHecke idempotent applies ∂_{w0}. This is how thick-calculus and Serre-relation computations go, as in KLMS and in Khovanov–Lauda/Rouquier. The specific propositions were not verified.
- **Floer origin.** If the deformed algebra arises from Floer theory (ADLSZ), then corrections to differentials of twisted complexes counting holomorphic polygons naturally organise as tree sums (A∞ structure maps). This is speculative.

### Gaps
- Exact proposition numbers were not verified for: Rouquier's polynomial realization (§3.2.2) and faithfulness; KL's faithfulness theorem (§2.3, likely Thm. 2.5); the relation τ f − s(f) τ = ∂f for equal labels. ar5iv truncated both papers.
- Not checked: Rouquier, "Quiver Hecke algebras and 2-Lie algebras", arXiv:1112.3619; Khovanov–Lauda II, arXiv:0804.2080; Brundan–Kleshchev isomorphism, arXiv:0808.2032; Maksimau–Stroppel; Webster's weighted KLR deformations. In particular, I could not confirm any paper that defines a KLR deformation with the **specific relation xψ − ψx = r_i**. It might be described as "the Rees algebra of the length filtration", "nil affine Hecke with parameter", or "loop-rotation / ħ-equivariant" (graded affine Hecke-type) deformation.
- I found **no** KLR or quiver Hecke paper where Demazure operators applied to a product ∏_{e∈E(G)} Q_e over a graph yield spanning-tree or Kirchhoff sums.
- Unverified pointer: Bressler–Evens, "The Schubert calculus, braid relations and generalized cohomology", Trans. AMS 1990. Divided-difference operators for a general formal group law satisfy braid relations only with correction terms, and the formal affine Demazure algebras of Hoffnung–Malagón-López–Savage–Zainoulline (arXiv:1208.4114) write braid relations with lower-order correction terms. Both are another classical instance of a "braid relation failing by lower-order terms", but neither was read here.

## Q4. Matrix-tree theorems, Kirchhoff and Symanzik polynomials, arborescences, orientations with out-degree conditions: connections to KLR, nilHecke, Schubert calculus and deformation theory

### Takeaway
The observed cyclic correction Σ_T (∏_{e∈T} c_e)(∏_{e∉T} Q_e), with c_e = r t, is **identically a (rescaled) first Symanzik / Kirchhoff polynomial**. By the matrix-tree theorem it equals **∏_e Q_e · det(reduced Laplacian of G with conductances c_e/Q_e, rooted at the moving strand)**. The "one-cycle" generalisation with an explicit combinatorial model is the **Forman–Kenyon cycle-rooted spanning forest (CRSF) expansion** of connection-Laplacian determinants, including a Dirichlet version rooted at a boundary set S. The closest formula found that literally applies **divided-difference/Demazure-type symmetrisation to products over graph edges** and gets tree combinatorics is **Postnikov–Petrov divided symmetrization**. Nothing found links these directly to KLR algebras.

### Cited Findings
- **First Symanzik polynomial.** U = Σ_{T spanning tree} ∏_{e ∉ T} x_e, equal to a minor of the Laplacian (matrix-tree theorem). The Kirchhoff polynomial Σ_T ∏_{e∈T} x_e is related to it by x_e ↦ 1/x_e. Bogner–Weinzierl also cover the all-minors matrix-tree theorem, deletion–contraction and Dodgson identities. — [Bogner–Weinzierl, "Feynman graph polynomials", arXiv:1002.3458](https://arxiv.org/abs/1002.3458); see also [Weinzierl, "Feynman Graphs", arXiv:1301.6918](https://arxiv.org/abs/1301.6918)
- **Grassmann/Berezin proofs.** Abdesselam, "The Grassmann–Berezin calculus and theorems of the matrix-tree type", arXiv:math/0306396 (Adv. Appl. Math. 2004), proves (i) the "massive" all-minors matrix-tree theorem (essentially Moon's, with no row/column-sum condition) and (ii) a version for non-symmetric matrices with zero column sums, with Pfaffian-tree and hyperpfaffian-cactus results, all using fermionic integration. — [arXiv:math/0306396](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0306396). Related: "Grassmann integral representation for spanning hyperforests", [arXiv:0706.1509](https://arxiv.org/abs/0706.1509); Sokal, "The multivariate Tutte polynomial (alias Potts model)", [arXiv:math/0503607](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0503607).
- **Kenyon, "Spanning forests and the vector bundle Laplacian", arXiv:1001.4028, Ann. Probab. 39 (2011).**
  - **Thm. 1 (Forman):** for a line bundle, det Δ = Σ_{CRSF} ∏_{cycles}(2 − w − w^{-1}), where w is the monodromy. Equivalently, det Δ = Σ_{oriented CRSF} ∏(1 − w).
  - **Thm. 5:** with edge weights, det Δ = Σ_{CRSF} ∏_e C(e) ∏_{cycles}(2 − w − 1/w).
  - **Thm. 6 (directed, Forman):** det Δ = Σ_{OCRSF} ∏_{bush edges} c(e) ∏_γ C(γ)(1 − w(γ)).
  - **Thm. 7 (Dirichlet):** for a boundary set S, det Δ_{G,S} = Σ over "essential CRSFs", in which each component is **either a tree containing exactly one vertex of S, or a cycle-rooted tree containing no vertex of S**, with weight ∏_{cycles}(2 − w − w^{-1}). It reduces to the matrix-tree theorem when |S| = 1 and the connection is trivial.
  - Thm. 9: the SL_2 version with weights 2 − tr(w).

- **Divided symmetrization.**
  - Petrov, "Combinatorial and probabilistic formulae for divided symmetrization", arXiv:1512.07136, defines DS_G(f) = Sym(f / ∏_{(x,y)∈E, x<y}(x − y)) for a graph G. For a **tree** T with deg f = n − 1 the value is constant, and **Thm. 2** gives DS_T(monomial C) = sign(C) × #(C-acceptable permutations). The sign counts "inversive" edges, defined via vertex weights w(x) ≥ −1 of total −1.
  - Path case: Lemma 3, Thm. 4 (cyclic sums), Thms. 5–6 (a coin game on a cycle C_{n+d}). Thm. 4 recovers **Postnikov's Cor. 6.5** in "Permutohedra, associahedra, and beyond", IMRN 2009 no. 6, 1026–1106.

### Inferences
- **Algebraic identity (exact; elementary).** Let c_e be the tree-edge weight (r t)_e and Q_e the bigon polynomial. Then Σ_T ∏_{e∈T} c_e ∏_{e∉T} Q_e = (∏_{e∈E} Q_e)·K_G(c/Q) = (∏_{e∈E} c_e)·U_G(Q/c). By the (weighted, rooted) matrix-tree theorem this equals **∏_e Q_e · det L_{root}(w_e = c_e/Q_e)**, the determinant of the Laplacian with the root row/column removed. The observed "dotted part for cyclic G" is thus a **determinant** (a polynomial version of the Kirchhoff/Symanzik polynomial), which suggests a Koszul/Berezin-type explanation à la Abdesselam. The signs in the observed tree sum must be matched to a signed or directed Laplacian (Abdesselam's non-symmetric case, or Kenyon Thm. 6); this has not been checked.
- **Why "each non-root vertex picks an out-edge" is matrix-tree combinatorics.** Use the twisted Leibniz rule ∂_p(fg) = ∂_p(f) g + s_p(f) ∂_p(g), and note that ∂_p(Q_e) is a constant for linear Q_e incident to p. Applying ∏_{p≠root} ∂_p to ∏_e Q_e makes each non-root vertex p **choose one incident edge** to differentiate. A doubly chosen linear edge dies, so the choice is injective. The chosen edges form a functional digraph with |V| − 1 edges, whose components are either **trees rooted at the root** or **unicycles (cycle-rooted trees)**. This is exactly the expansion in the standard combinatorial proof of the matrix-tree theorem, where the cycle-containing terms cancel. In Forman–Kenyon (Thm. 7 with S = {root}) the cycle-rooted components survive with weight (1 − w)(1 − w^{-1}) when a "monodromy" w ≠ 1 is present. So:
  - if the reflections s_p in the twisted Leibniz rule produce cancellation, only spanning trees survive (matrix-tree);
  - if they act like a nontrivial holonomy around a cycle, **cycle-rooted terms appear**.

  This is a **speculative but precise** candidate model for the "single-cycle rule". The orientation expansion ∏_e(t_e x_{tail} + t'_e x_{head}) = Σ_{orientations} ∏ x_v^{outdeg(v)} supplies the "surplus out-edges become dots" bookkeeping, in the spirit of Alon–Tarsi.
- **Demazure and divided symmetrization (exact, standard).** Since w(Δ) = (−1)^{ℓ(w)} Δ for Δ = ∏_{i<j}(x_i − x_j), the top Demazure operator satisfies ∂_{w0}(g) = Σ_w w(g/Δ) = Sym(g/Δ). Hence DS_G(f) = ±∂_{w0}( f · ∏_{(i<j)∉E}(x_i − x_j) ). Petrov's Thm. 2 is therefore a closed **tree-combinatorial formula for a Demazure operator applied to a product of linear "edge" forms over the complement graph**. It is the nearest published "Demazure(∏ edge polynomials) = tree sum" statement found. The observed operator ∏_p ∂_p (commuting transpositions on disjoint pairs) differs from ∂_{w0}.

#### Ranking of the closest known formulas to the observed structure
1. **Rouquier's (i,j,i) braid relation:** braid defect = divided difference of Q_ij. This gives the edge weight r_i t_ij exactly. [arXiv:0812.5023 §3.2.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/0812.5023)
2. **Matrix-tree / first Symanzik polynomial:** spanning tree × ∏_{e∉T} Q_e is exactly U_G or K_G with edge variables Q_e/(rt)_e, i.e. a reduced-Laplacian determinant. [arXiv:1002.3458](https://arxiv.org/abs/1002.3458)
3. **Forman–Kenyon Dirichlet CRSF expansion:** rooted trees plus cycle-rooted components. This is the template for one-cycle corrections. [arXiv:1001.4028 Thm. 7](https://arxiv.org/abs/1001.4028)
4. **Kontsevich–Soibelman / Markl planar-tree transfer formulas:** the template for tree-indexed corrections built from a homotopy. [math/0011041 §6.4](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0011041); [math/0401007 Prop. 6](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0401007)

## Q5. Deformation-theoretic framing: lifting resolutions and complexes of projectives from the associated graded to a filtered or deformed algebra; perturbation series and uniqueness

### Takeaway
Three established results frame the observed phenomenon:
1. **Lowen's characteristic-morphism obstruction.** The failure d_0^2 ≠ 0 at first order represents χ(c)_M ∈ Ext^2(M,M), the image of the Hochschild class of the deformation.
2. **Rickard's lifting theorem.** Tilting or "rigid" complexes lift uniquely over complete local deformations.
3. **Braverman–Gaitsgory / Positselski nonhomogeneous Koszul duality.** Lower-order terms in relations become curvature, and PBW follows from a deformation argument.

Together these give existence and uniqueness of the corrected differential when the relevant Hom groups vanish in the graded degree, with the correction obtained by solving an MC equation order by order. No single source gives the explicit tree formula.

### Cited Findings
- **Lowen, "Hochschild cohomology, the characteristic morphism and derived deformations", arXiv:0707.2602, Compositio Math. 144 (2008).** For c ∈ HH^2(A) corresponding to a first-order deformation A_c and M ∈ D^b(A), the element χ_A(c)_M ∈ Ext^2_A(M,M) is **exactly the obstruction** to deforming M to an object of D^b(A_c). Earlier work, Lowen arXiv:math/0407019, gives obstruction theory for lifting complexes to flat nilpotent deformations of abelian categories, with miniversal derived deformations. The characteristic morphism HH^*(A) → Z^*(D^b(A)) is from Lowen–Van den Bergh. Toda (2009) states the analogous result for perfect complexes on varieties. — [arXiv:0707.2602](https://arxiv.org/abs/0707.2602v1); [Cambridge PDF](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/34F48C10C62CD14FAFC728F878269C4A/S0010437X08003655a.pdf/hochschild-cohomology-the-characteristic-morphism-and-derived-deformations.pdf); [arXiv:math/0407019](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0407019v1); [arXiv:math/0405227](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0405227); [arXiv:2408.03027](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.03027)
- **Rickard, "Lifting theorems for tilting complexes", J. Algebra (1991), Thm. 3.3.** For R a complete Noetherian local ring with residue field k and Ã an R-algebra deformation of A, a tilting complex for A lifts to a **unique** tilting complex T̃ over Ã with T̃ ⊗_R k ≅ T. This is quoted via Schaps-related notes and a 2023 paper, not read in the original. — [notes on Schaps' tiltings (Benson's site)](https://homepages.abdn.ac.uk/d.j.benson/pages/papers/s/schaps/Tiltings.pdf); [arXiv:2303.04893](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.04893)
- **Braverman–Gaitsgory, "Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt theorem for quadratic algebras of Koszul type", arXiv:hep-th/9411113, J. Algebra 181 (1996).** Take a nonhomogeneous quadratic algebra U whose associated homogeneous algebra A is Koszul. Conditions (I) and (J) (J generalises the Jacobi identity) imply PBW, gr U ≅ A. The proof constructs a graded deformation interpolating between A and U and uses Hochschild cohomology. Polishchuk–Positselski proved the same independently. — [arXiv:hep-th/9411113](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/9411113)
- **Shepler–Witherspoon.** Survey "Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt theorems", arXiv:1404.6497 (MSRI Publ. 67), compares homological approaches with composition–diamond lemmas. Related papers: "Deforming group actions on Koszul algebras", arXiv:1606.07135; "Deformation theory and Hopf actions on Koszul algebras", arXiv:2504.14397 (twisted tensor product resolutions K_S ⊗_τ B_H; which Hochschild 2-cocycles lift to PBW deformations). Walton–Witherspoon, Algebra & Number Theory 8:7 (2014). — [arXiv:1404.6497](https://arxiv.org/abs/1404.6497); [arXiv:1606.07135](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.07135); [arXiv:2504.14397](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.14397v2); [ANT 2014](https://msp.org/ant/2014/8-7/ant-v8-n7-p05-p.pdf)
- **Crainic, Remark 2.3(iii) and §3.7.** With a filtration lowered by δ, the perturbation series converges formally, and contractions in degrees k, k+1 survive perturbation. — [arXiv:math/0403266](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0403266)
- **Eisenbud–Shamash.** This is the commutative prototype of "lift a resolution and correct d_0^2 ≠ 0 with higher homotopies" (see Q2). — [arXiv:2212.06999](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.06999)

### Inferences
- **Proposed general mechanism.** This is standard deformation theory assembled from the sources above; I found no single reference that states it.
  1. Lift each projective P^n from A_0 = A/(r,t) to A over k[[r,t]], or k[r,t] in the graded setting. This is unobstructed because idempotent-generated projectives lift.
  2. Lift d_0 termwise. Then κ := d_0^2 ∈ m·End^2_A(P), and its leading part represents Lowen's χ(c)_P ∈ Ext^2(P_0,P_0).
  3. Solve the MC equation κ + [d_0, x] + x^2 = 0 for x ∈ m·End^1. With a contracting homotopy h of (End_{A_0}(P_0), [d_0, −]) away from its cohomology, the recursion x = −h(κ + x^2) gives x as a **sum over rooted binary trees with leaves κ and internal edges h**. The obstruction at each order lies in H^2(End(P_0)) = Ext^2, and the ambiguity lies in H^1 = Ext^1.
  4. In a **fully graded** setting with r, t of positive degree, the relevant graded pieces of Ext^1 and Ext^2 often vanish for degree reasons. This gives **existence and uniqueness** (Rickard-style), consistent with the observed uniqueness of the corrected differential.
- **Curvature.** The triple-point failure r_i t_ij·(identity diagram) is a "constant term" in a nonhomogeneous relation. In Positselski's nonhomogeneous Koszul duality such constant parts become the **curvature** of the dual CDG object. Braverman–Gaitsgory's (I)/(J) conditions are the obstruction-vanishing that makes the filtered lift flat (PBW). This is the conceptual reason "corrected differential = undeformed differential + MC solution" is the right framing.
- **Associated graded versus filtered.** Rouquier's statement that quiver Hecke algebras are filtered with associated graded polynomial ⋊ nilHecke (Q3) suggests reading r_i and t_ij as Rees parameters: d_0 is the resolution over the graded or degenerate algebra, and d is its filtered lift. Anick-type and algebraic-Morse resolutions build such lifts by "zig-zag path" sums. Those are path sums again, not tree sums; this is from memory and not verified.
